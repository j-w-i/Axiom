# The wire

Types: `engine/crates/proto`. Why: decision 0003. One JSON object per line, UTF-8, at most 8 MiB.

1. Connect to the Unix socket named in `<data>/run/endpoint`, on every system. The name is the host's to choose, and may change while it runs; nobody computes it. A host whose token, endpoint or socket is removed or replaced puts it back within a few seconds, or stops in order if it cannot. On Unix the host drops peers with another uid.
2. First line, at most 4 KiB: `hello` with the token in `<data>/run/token` and the protocol number. Anything else, a wrong token or another protocol gets one error and the connection closes. Silence closes it after 5 s. Over 64 clients, `hello` is answered `busy`; over 32 connections without it, the oldest of them is closed.
3. `{"id":1,"method":"session.list","params":{}}` → `{"id":1,"result":…}` or `{"id":1,"error":{"code":…,"message":…}}`. An unreadable request is answered with `id` 0 if it has none. Errors do not repeat what was sent. While more answers and events wait than the host keeps for one client, it reads no more requests. A client that ends its side of the connection is answered for 2 s more.
4. `session.follow {session, after}` is answered, then `{"session":…,"events":[…]}` messages follow: history after `after` at the client's pace, then live, in order, nothing twice. History has holes where ended items' deltas were folded. A second follow of a session on one connection replaces the first: its answer comes before any of its events, and nothing of the first comes after that answer.
5. `{"session":…,"resync":N}` means the client fell behind, or, with `N`=0 and only then, that its `after` is ahead of this history. Nothing more comes for that session until it follows again: from its last `seq`, or with 0 from 0 after discarding what it has.
6. `{"session":…,"fault":"words"}` means the host cannot read or write the session. Nothing more comes for it; a client shows the words and does not follow again by itself. Asking for it (`session.follow`, `replay.start`) is a new try, answered with error `internal` and words while it does not work. `host.status` lists `faults` as `[{session, words}]` and, if a check of the journal's file after an unclean end found damage, `damaged` in words.
7. `session.list {limit, offset}` (200 and 0 if absent, at most 1000) is an array, last changed first. `running` means that this host runs something in it.
8. Over 128 runs at once, or 128 MiB of scripts held for them, `replay.start` is answered `busy`.
9. `host.shutdown` is refused with `busy` while agents run, unless `force`. A host that stops starts nothing, ends its runs, writes how they ended, sends it, and closes. `SIGTERM`, `SIGINT` and `SIGHUP` do the same with force.
10. What was sent is in the journal. `item.ended.text` is authoritative and replaces the deltas before it. A prompt's and a tool's `item.started`, `session.created`, `session.titled`, `agent.added`, `run.started` and `request.*` survive power loss once sent or answered; the rest survives the host.
11. Readers ignore unknown fields and keep unknown event types in sequence; an unknown enum value reads as `unknown`.
12. `connection.list`, `connection.add {name, provider, url?, secret?}` and `connection.remove {connection}`: the ways to reach providers, any number for each. A secret goes one way: no answer, event, error or file holds it; `secret` says that there is one, `lasts` that the system's store keeps it. `model.list {connection}` asks the provider, and answers what it says.
13. `workspace.look {path}` gives a folder's words for agents (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`), for a person to read. `agent.add {session, name, execution, access, instructions}` has those followed that a person names by their digest; other content is not followed, and that is said. It needs a session with a workspace (a folder the host can open, without Axiom's own files or what signs a person in), with the harness `axiom` a connection and a model. Another harness (`harness.list` says which there are, found without running any) is a program of the person's own: it has no connection, its model may be its own choice, and `trust` lets the folder's own settings for it take effect; it is started when it is first spoken to, after a `request.opened` that shows its command. An agent keeps its harness. `agent.set` changes execution or access between runs (`busy` during one), and ends what had been allowed for the session. Both are events (`agent.added`, `agent.changed`).
14. `agent.prompt {session, agent, text, key?}` starts a run, or is heard by the one that is on before its next step (`queued`). What was said too late for a run begins another, unless a person stopped it: then it waits until they speak again. Nothing said is lost over a stop or a restart. `key` (at most 100 bytes) names what is said: again under the same name, it is answered as before and heard once, as long as the host runs. A prompt's `item.started` has the `name` of the agent that said it, if it is not a person's. Answered when the run is in the journal.
15. `request.opened` with `answerable: live` can be answered by `request.answer {session, request, answer, digest}` with `once`, `session` or `deny`. `digest` is the event's: an answer to anything but what was shown is refused, and the question stays open (SI-2). `item` is the tool call it is about.
17. `workspace.changes {session}` gives what has been changed in the session's folder since its last commit: `repository`, `branch`, `files` (`new`, `changed`, `removed`, `conflict`), a unified `diff`, and whether that is the `whole` of it. Git runs in the sandbox, so not on Windows.
16. `audit.read {after, limit}` gives the record of what was done with authority; each entry holds the digest of the one before it.

Lengths, in bytes as encoded in JSON, kept by the host before it journals: text of an item 1 MiB, its arguments and its result 256 KiB each, an approval's subject and a run's error 64 KiB, scopes and answers 4 KiB, a workspace 4 KiB, titles, names and an approval's `what` 500. Text that is cut ends with `[N more bytes were not kept]` (while its item runs, with `[more is not kept]`; after a damaged part of it, with words in brackets). Arguments and results over the limit, or nested deeper than 64, are left out, and `args_cut` on `item.started` and `result_cut` on `item.ended` say how many bytes they had; these fields are absent otherwise. A request that can be answered (`answerable` other than `no`) is never cut: over a limit it is refused where it is made. `session.create` refuses a title or workspace over its limit. A message of events holds about 256 KiB, or one longer event.
