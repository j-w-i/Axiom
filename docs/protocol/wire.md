# The wire

Types: `engine/crates/proto`. Why: decision 0003. One JSON object per line, UTF-8, at most 8 MiB.

1. Connect to the Unix socket named in `<data>/run/endpoint`, on every system. The name is the host's to choose, and may change while it runs; nobody computes it. A host whose token, endpoint or socket is removed or replaced puts it back within a few seconds, or stops in order if it cannot. On Unix the host drops peers with another uid.
2. First line, at most 4 KiB: `hello` with the token in `<data>/run/token` and the protocol number. Anything else, a wrong token or another protocol gets one error and the connection closes. Silence closes it after 5 s. Over 64 clients, `hello` is answered `busy`; over 32 connections without it, the oldest of them is closed.
3. `{"id":1,"method":"session.list","params":{}}` → `{"id":1,"result":…}` or `{"id":1,"error":{"code":…,"message":…}}`. An unreadable request is answered with `id` 0 if it has none. Errors do not repeat what was sent. While more answers and events wait than the host keeps for one client, it reads no more requests. A client that ends its side of the connection is answered for 2 s more.
4. `session.follow {session, after}` is answered, then `{"session":…,"events":[…]}` messages follow: history after `after` at the client's pace, then live, in order, nothing twice. History has holes where ended items' deltas were folded. A second follow of a session on one connection replaces the first: its answer comes before any of its events, and nothing of the first comes after that answer.
5. `{"session":…,"resync":N}` means the client fell behind, or, with `N`=0 and only then, that its `after` is ahead of this history. Nothing more comes for that session until it follows again: from its last `seq`, or with 0 from 0 after discarding what it has.
6. `{"session":…,"fault":"words"}` means the host cannot read or write the session. Nothing more comes for it; a client shows the words and does not follow again by itself. Asking for it (`session.follow`, `replay.start`) is a new try, answered with error `internal` and words while it does not work. `host.status` lists `faults` as `[{session, words}]` and, if a check of the journal's file after an unclean end found damage, `damaged` in words.
7. `session.list {limit, offset}` (200 and 0 if absent, at most 1000) is an array, last changed first. `running` means that this host runs something in it.
8. Over 128 runs at once, or 128 MiB of scripts held for them, `replay.start` is answered `busy`.
9. `host.shutdown` is refused with `busy` while agents run, unless `force`. A host that stops starts nothing, ends its runs, writes how they ended, sends it, and closes. `SIGTERM`, `SIGINT` and `SIGHUP` do the same with force.
10. What was sent is in the journal. `item.ended.text` is authoritative and replaces the deltas before it. A prompt's and a tool's `item.started`, `session.created`, `session.titled`, `agent.added`, `run.started` and `request.*` survive power loss once sent or answered; the rest survives the host.
11. Readers ignore unknown fields and keep unknown event types in sequence; an unknown enum value reads as `unknown`.
12. `connection.list`, `connection.add {name, provider, url?, secret?}` and `connection.remove {connection}`: the ways to reach providers, any number for each. A secret goes one way: no answer, event, error or file holds it; `secret` says that there is one, `lasts` that the system's store keeps it. With the provider that `harness.list` names as a harness's `accounts` it is another account of that program: no key and no address, a folder of its own, and `sign_in`, the command a person signs in with themselves. An agent that is moved to another account begins anew there. `model.list {connection}` asks the provider, and answers what it says: of each model its `context`, the `efforts` it can be told, as its provider calls them, the one of them it tries at when told none (`default_effort`, where its provider, its page or its program says), whether it takes being told to think as much as it needs (`adaptive`: then it is told so, always; it is no choice) and how fast it can be beside the standard, for a price (`speeds`). What nobody says is not there. An agent's execution has `effort` and `speed`; what its provider and model have no place for is refused when it is set up. An account of the provider `chatgpt` is made by signing in only; `problem` says why it cannot be used until somebody signs in again; removing it signs out of it as well, and `ended` says whether that is certain.
13. `workspace.look {path}` gives a folder's words for agents (`AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`), for a person to read. `agent.add {session, name, execution, access, instructions}` has those followed that a person names by their digest; other content is not followed, and that is said. It needs a session with a workspace (a folder the host can open, without Axiom's own files or what signs a person in), with the harness `axiom` a connection and a model. Another harness (`harness.list` says which there are, found without running any) is a program of the person's own: it has no connection, its model may be its own choice, and `trust` lets the folder's own settings for it take effect; it is started when it is first spoken to, after a `request.opened` that shows its command. An agent keeps its harness. `agent.set` changes execution or access between runs (`busy` during one), and ends what had been allowed for the session. Both are events (`agent.added`, `agent.changed`).
14. `agent.prompt {session, agent, text, key?}` starts a run, or is heard by the one that is on before its next step (`queued`). What was said too late for a run begins another, unless a person stopped it: then it waits until they speak again. Nothing said is lost over a stop or a restart. `key` (at most 100 bytes) names what is said: again under the same name, it is answered as before and heard once, as long as the host runs. A prompt's `item.started` has the `name` of the agent that said it, if it is not a person's. Answered when the run is in the journal.
15. `request.opened` with `answerable: live` can be answered by `request.answer {session, request, answer, digest}` with `once`, `session` or `deny`. `digest` is the event's: an answer to anything but what was shown is refused, and the question stays open (SI-2). With `deny`, `words` (at most 4 KiB) say why: the model that asked reads them with its refusal; a program that works with its own tools is told after its turn, as if its person had said it. `item` is the tool call it is about.
17. `workspace.changes {session}` gives what has been changed in the session's folder since its last commit: `repository`, `branch`, `files` (`new`, `changed`, `removed`, `conflict`), a unified `diff`, and whether that is the `whole` of it. Git runs in the sandbox, so not on Windows.
18. `peer.hello {grant}` instead of `hello` is for the program an agent is: its answer names the `tools` that its key opens by `peer.call {tool, args}`, and it opens nothing else, as long as its attempt lives. That is `agent`: what a program does with its own tools, it is given no other for (decision 0017). The key is in a file of mode 0600 that `axiom-host --mcp FILE` reads, the Model Context Protocol server such a program is told to start. A call ends when its connection does.
16. `audit.read {after, limit}` gives the record of what was done with authority; each entry holds the digest of the one before it.
19. `signin.begin {connection?}` answers `{signin, url}`: an address for the person's browser, which the host does not open, and which the browser comes back from by itself (decision 0013). With `connection` it is that account again. `signin.state {signin}` is `waiting`, `finishing`, `done` with its `connection`, or `failed` with a `problem` in words; the last 16 are remembered as long as the host runs. `signin.cancel {signin}` ends one that waits. Four wait at once, ten minutes each; more are answered `busy`.
20. `agent.add` with `apart` (and `agent.added` says so): the agent works in a copy of the folder, made when it is first spoken to, so that the first `agent.prompt` may take a while (decision 0014). `copy.changes {session, agent}` gives `made`; `files` (`new`, `changed`, `removed`), each with the `digest` and `run` of the copy's, the `digest` of the folder's as `there`, its `diff` if that can be shown, `clash` if the folder's own has changed too, `unsettled` if it had when the copy was brought up to date and the agent has not touched its own since, `never` with why, `runs` if it runs when someone builds, `others` that have changed it in their copies; whether these are the `whole`; how much git `ignored` or is too `odd` to list; and the last that can be put `back`, by `id` and number of `files`. `copy.bring {session, agent, files: [{path, digest?, run?, there?}]}` brings in exactly those and answers `brought` and `left` with why; `copy.back {session, agent, back}` puts that back and answers `back` and `left`. `copy.renew` and `copy.remove {lose?}` are `busy` while the agent works; the first answers `taken`, `clashes`, `left` and what to `say` to the agent.
21. `host.waiting` gives what waits for a person in all sessions: first the questions (`need`: `asks`), the longest first, each with its `request` and `what`; then what agents came to that nobody has seen (`failed`, `limit`, then `finished`). Each has `session` and its `title`, `agent` and its `name`, the beginning of its `subject`, and since when (`at`). A question is answered where it is asked, by `request.answer`, with the digest of its `request.opened`. An end is kept two seconds after it, if nothing of it went on and its person (not another agent) began it; it is kept over a restart, until `agent.seen {session, agent}`, or until the agent is at work again. `{"waiting":N}` is sent to every client that has said hello when any of it has changed; of several changes, the last is told.
22. What an agent was told to do by another reaches that one by itself and once, marked as another agent's word: at the end of its own run, or, if it is not at work, by a run that begins for it, at most 8 in a row without a person's word. One that failed, or that has been stopped, or after the host was away, hears it when it is spoken to next.
23. `agent.branch {session, agent, item, name, files?}` adds an agent that goes on from another's conversation (decision 0015): as it was before `item`, if that is a prompt of a person's that began a run, or after it, if it is the last answer of a run that was completed. At nothing else, and not of a program, which keeps its own conversation. It runs on the same and may do the same, and is told nothing by this. `agent.added` then has `from {agent, item, with}`: the other's items up to that one are its own too, `with` it or without. `files`, for an agent in a copy: it gets a copy with the files as they were there, refused in words if one of them is not to be had any more; without, a new copy when it is spoken to. `branch.look {session, agent, item}` says first whether it can be: an error if not, otherwise `apart` and whether the `files` are kept.
24. `host.limits` gives the limits this host keeps, for a person to read: each has what it is a limit `of` (those of one kind come together), `what` it limits in words, `most` (absent: there is none) and its `unit`, `count`, `bytes` or `seconds`. They come from what they are kept by, and not all limits are among them.
25. `usage` says what was used: of every answer of a model, also one that broke off (`cut`) and one that summarised the conversation (`aside`). `on` is what it was used on, as it was then (`lane`: `key`, `plan`, `program`, `open`; `model` asked for, `answered` said to have answered); `of` what `usage` covers (`answer`, `turn`; `session` is a running total, not to be added up; absent: not said), and `cost` and `totals`, by model, say so for themselves. Each figure has its provenance. A cost is there only if a provider or a program says one: nothing here knows a price. How the tokens of a program add up is as its own documentation has it; `on.harness` says which program that is.
26. `program.list` gives what a person has said of how programs are started (decision 0016), `program.set {harness?, account?, name?, path?, args, vars}` says it anew, of a program or of one account of it; without `harness` it adds one that speaks ACP, with a name and a whole path, and answers with what it is called as a harness (`harness.list` has it, with `own`). What is refused, and why, is said in words. Each of `vars` has a `name` and a `value`; one that is `secret`, or whose name says so, goes one way, as a connection's secret does, and without a value it stays as it is. It is true of the next start, whose question shows all of it but what is secret. `program.remove {harness, account?}` takes it away; with an account goes what was said of it.
27. `todo` is what an agent says that it means to do: `steps`, each with a `state` (`todo`, `doing`, `done`), and `text` in words, all of it in place of what it said before; with neither there is none any more. An agent of Axiom's own says it with the tool `todo`, a program as its protocol has it. It is a claim: nothing waits for it and nothing follows from it.
28. An agent with `plan` (`agent.add`, `agent.set`; `agent.added` and `agent.changed` say so) only reads, and is told to hand in a plan with the tool `plan`: an item of the kind `plan`, Markdown of at most 64 KiB. One of Axiom's own; a program is refused. Handing one in allows nothing. `plan.accept {session, agent, item, access, text?}` accepts the last one, between the agent's runs, as it is or as `text` has it: the agent may do what `access` says from then on, is not asked for a plan any more, and is told to begin; answered as `agent.prompt`. Another access, set with `agent.set`, ends it too. Why a plan is not accepted is said to the agent like anything else.
29. `model.known` gives what providers and programs have said of their models, the last of each, as it was kept, with when (`at`; 0: from a program's documentation). Nobody is asked, and no program is started for it: one tells its models when it is started for something else. `model.about {connection, model}` gives what is known of one model, also of one that is in no list. A list goes with its account or its program.

Lengths, in bytes as encoded in JSON, kept by the host before it journals: text of an item 1 MiB, its arguments and its result 256 KiB each, an approval's subject and a run's error 64 KiB, scopes and answers 4 KiB, a workspace 4 KiB, titles, names and an approval's `what` 500. Text that is cut ends with `[N more bytes were not kept]` (while its item runs, with `[more is not kept]`; after a damaged part of it, with words in brackets). Arguments and results over the limit, or nested deeper than 64, are left out, and `args_cut` on `item.started` and `result_cut` on `item.ended` say how many bytes they had; these fields are absent otherwise. A request that can be answered (`answerable` other than `no`) is never cut: over a limit it is refused where it is made. `session.create` refuses a title or workspace over its limit. A message of events holds about 256 KiB, or one longer event.
